# ReleaseProof · commercial decision · 24 November 2026

**Ship the free review experiment for owner consideration. Defer the proposed US$9 offline Pro edition.** The prototype handles an agent's cross-tool completion claims before a human accepts a release. Its useful distinction is being able to filter reported passes with missing evidence and perform a narrow, visible check on supplied files. The browser already works offline after the initial load. Offline access alone gives a weak reason to pay.

| Free alternative | What its public documentation establishes | Where it fits the owner's job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RepoPrompt Community Edition and Repomix | RepoPrompt supports reviewable handoffs and context curation. Repomix packs repository context. | Good starting points for preparing an agent's next session. ReleaseProof takes the receiving owner's acceptance criteria and evidence as its inputs. [RepoPrompt](https://repoprompt.com/) · [Repomix](https://repomix.com/) |

| Pixelmatch | A free library compares pixels from two images. | Prefer it for image regression with a known baseline. ReleaseProof checks declared dimensions and missing references. It does not measure visual similarity. [Pixelmatch](https://github.com/mapbox/pixelmatch) |

**Evidence level.** Official public pages were read directly. This is a documented-feature comparison, not a timed head-to-head evaluation. The SURV-001 fixture preserves all 15 historical outcomes. Two supplied screenshots allow exact dimension checks. Other historical PASS observations remain report-author claims in this review. A missing attachment is a review question; some criteria are better supported by a log or a repeatable test.

**Unresolved demand.** A solo owner could do this with a text editor, file browser and image inspector. Converting a report to JSON could consume more time than the viewer saves. No interview, purchase commitment or private traffic data establishes that people want another tool. Community contributions and free pack downloads supply no software-demand evidence.

**Next experiment.** Let Codex generate two equal-length fictional handoffs, each with one missing screenshot, one wrong-sized screenshot and one unsupported behavioural PASS. Publish neither paid checkout nor a Pro preorder. After owner approval, invite three volunteer reviewers through one existing developer channel. Alternate starting method. Give each reviewer one handoff with their usual tools and the other in ReleaseProof. Record aggregate author preparation time, reviewer time, gaps correctly identified and any false implication that a behaviour was tested. Include the cost of JSON preparation in the time comparison. Cap owner coordination at ten minutes.

Continue free iteration if at least two of three find all three gaps with the viewer, nobody interprets size verification as a behavioural PASS, and median total time falls by 25% or more. Ask which repeated task would justify US$9. Only scope a paid feature if two independent users name the same unmet need, accept that price, and a free existing tool fails their workflow. Otherwise keep the viewer small or stop. These are proposed decision thresholds.
